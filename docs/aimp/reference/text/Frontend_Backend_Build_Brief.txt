AiMoneyPrinter
Customer services and frontend +
backend build brief
Product scope: Proposed customer experience based on the Master Business Plan, the AMP backlog and
the Figma agent maps. The inventory includes the first release, later improvements, expansion models and
the platform features that affect the interface. Release labels are design and build priorities. Confirm
shipped capabilities before enabling them in the app.

Design goal: Let a customer with minimal time, budget and technical experience choose a business and let
AiMoneyPrinter build, launch and operate it. Ask for the exact input needed when an action depends on the
owner. Keep everything else moving.

Audience: Store owner, their shoppers and subscribers, and the AiMoneyPrinter team that handles
operational exceptions. The Chief of Staff development dashboard is an internal surface. The Business
Operator and its activity are customer-facing.

before a store exists. Route contextual screens from these five destinations. Desktop can use the same
destinations in a sidebar. Make Home the place to resume an unfinished build and see what needs the
owner today.

Automation controls and exceptions
The customer should see what happened, what is currently running and the exact action that would
unblock a task. Relevant examples include identity verification, expired account access, unlicensed assets,
risky copy, low credits, high-intent replies, refunds, a full calendar and requests for a person. An
unsubscribe cancels pending promotional follow-up. For temporary failures, show the next retry and saved
progress. For paused work, show the affected tasks and what is still running.

Design these key actions: Approve, Decline, Edit and review, Reconnect, Retry, Pause, Resume, Take
over, and Contact support. Show the affected store and the scope of the change. For spending, show the
amount, cap, time period, channel and how to pause. Approval and audit views should show the proposal,
decision, timestamp and outcome.

6. Design acceptance checklist
• First design flow: Onboarding five ideas select build progress product and store preview
   connect payments publish buyer checkout download owner’s first-sale view first campaign.

• First set of alternate screens: Product generation failed, payments pending, social connection expired,
   payment delayed or failed, delivery retry, expired download, consent withdrawn and owner approval
   required.

   context, provider connection card, status badge, approval card, order timeline, asset preview, campaign
   calendar row, lead or conversation card, report metric, empty state and failure panel.

• State coverage: Loading and long-running, draft, ready for review, scheduled, published, no data, partial
   data, paused, permission missing, blocked, failed, retrying, recovered, cancelled and completed. Use
   clear messages and recovery actions. Keep test and demo data distinguishable from live results.

• Responsive and accessible: Start at mobile width. Check desktop layouts for side-by-side comparisons,
   calendars, orders and analytics. Include keyboard navigation, focus, readable contrast, labelled fields
   and status that uses text as well as colour.

• Customer effort: Surface one primary next action and a way to inspect details. Ask only for the
   information needed for the active step. For independently progressing tasks, show status in the
   background so the owner can leave and return.

• Financial information: Distinguish gross sales, fees, refunds, payouts and estimated profit. Label
   scenario inputs and example data. Show provider readiness and fees where known.

• Design handoff: Mark each screen with its release tier and feature IDs. Annotate the trigger, who can act,
   what data appears, main interaction, success and failure, and the route that follows.
